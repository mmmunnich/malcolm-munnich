A snapshot bundles every raw box score XML and Trackman CSV you've uploaded into one downloadable file -- a backup, or a way to move your data to a different browser or computer. Loading a snapshot re-parses everything through the app's current logic (so any parsing fixes since your last snapshot apply automatically), and skips any game you already have stored. Games uploaded before this feature existed won't be in a snapshot until you re-upload them once, since their raw text wasn't kept until now.

Enter a batted ball's landing and/or contact data to see its live out probability. How this works: we take every real batted ball from this season already matched to a fielding outcome, find the ones most similar to what you entered (similarity = standardized distance in feature space -- Distance/Bearing/Hang Time for the Landing model, Exit Velo/Launch Angle/Direction for the Contact model), weight the closest matches more heavily, and Out Probability is simply the weighted share of those similar historical balls that were actually converted into outs. That's the whole model -- no hidden adjustments. Fill in whichever set of fields you have; only that model computes, and each is scored independently.

Takes a player's current counting stats and scales them to what they'd project to over a 162-team-game season, based on their own team's games played so far (162 ÷ team games played). Rate stats (AVG/OBP/SLG/ERA/etc.) don't change under prorating -- only counting stats do.

League-wide min, 10th percentile, median, 90th percentile, and max for every stat on the site -- both the ones pulled straight from your data and the ones this dashboard calculates (WAR, xBA, OAE, and so on). Pulled directly from the same leaderboard columns you already see, so nothing here can drift out of sync with the leaderboard itself.

MLB (Fangraphs-style) WAR includes a positional adjustment: a fixed runs-per-full-season value added or subtracted based on defensive spectrum, since an average defender at a scarce, demanding position (catcher, shortstop) provides more value than an average defender at an easy one (first base, DH) -- the fielding metric alone only measures performance relative to others at that same position, so without this, two players who both grade as "average" at their own spot would look equally valuable even though holding down shortstop is a lot harder to replace than holding down first base. This tool does NOT change your actual WAR numbers above -- it's a side-by-side preview so you can see exactly what applying the standard MLB scale would do to your specific league's rankings before deciding whether it's worth keeping on.

Player Name Aliases

For when the same player is recorded under two names that no general nickname rule could ever safely cover -- e.g. an initials-based nickname like "AJ" for "Armando", which has no phonetic relationship to the formal name at all (unlike Nick/Nicholas or Mike/Michael). Add both spellings below and every stat on the site will treat them as the same player. Team should match however it's spelled in your data.

Trackman Name Matching Check

Every distinct batter/pitcher name (and team) found in your uploaded Trackman files, and whether it matched a real player in your box scores. Use this to spot spelling or team-name mismatches -- e.g. a Trackman entry for "N. Nashiwa" won't match a box score's "Nick Nashiwa" automatically.
